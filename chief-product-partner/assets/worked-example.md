# Worked discovery example: [idea]

**SIMULATED:** Invented customers, counts, quotes, costs, tests, and decisions below show how the process works. They are not evidence for the live idea. Put checked public facts in a separate paragraph with dated primary-source links. Finish with the real project's current stage.

For each stage, use two short parts: **What we would do together** and **Sample output**. Use the same customer, problem, and evidence trail throughout. Do not jump from a fictional interview directly to a confident build decision.

1. **Frame:** First group, task, intended outcome, decision, and starting guesses.
2. **Understand:** Recent journey, distinct possible barriers, and observations that would separate them.
3. **Research:** Secondary source checks and first-hand work. Give selection, sample, period, and limits for all SIMULATED findings.
4. **Make sense of it:** Pattern, exceptions, strongest alternative explanation, and how the initial frame changed.
5. **State the bets:** Necessary beliefs and what would show each is false.
6. **Explore options:** At least two meaningfully different responses and their gains, costs, and constraints, including doing less if warranted.
7. **Test:** State decision rules before SIMULATED results. Use actual payment or behaviour in the fictional test when claiming willingness to pay; compare every result with its preset rule and note limits.
8. **Decide:** Proceed, revise, retest, research more, defer, or stop; strongest objection, next commitment, owner, and review trigger.
9. **Describe the first version, if warranted:** Short PRD linked to the fictional decision, with scope, success measures, payment failures or other edge cases, dependencies, and unverified conditions. If the fictional decision is stop or defer, show the decision brief instead of forcing a PRD.

End with **Where the real project stands:** name its actual stage, what is truly known, and the next evidence needed. Never copy SIMULATED findings into the live record.
