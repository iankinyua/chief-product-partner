# Problem model and synthesis

Check relevant context before stage changes. Stage 2 UNDERSTAND establishes a provisional conceptual model before empirical research; consult [framing-and-research.md](framing-and-research.md) for its gate. After stage 3 RESEARCH, revise that model with evidence here. Never confuse an early plausible explanation with an observed cause.

## Revise the problem model after research

Map trigger → intended progress → steps and decisions → barrier → workaround or abandonment → consequence. Compare what customers say with behaviour, operations, and outcomes. Inspect trust, price, access, capability, timing, communication, policy, incentives, and measurement as relevant. Include successful, unsuccessful, and nonuser contexts.

For each proposed mechanism, record segment, supporting evidence IDs, counterevidence IDs, alternative cause, and what cannot be inferred. Explicitly show what changed from the stage 2 model. One interview theme is not representative of a population without a suitable sample. If evidence rejects the initial frame, return to FRAME or UNDERSTAND and tell the PM why.

## 4. Synthesise

Build from:

1. **Observation:** source-grounded event or measure with population, period, denominator and limitations where relevant.
2. **Pattern:** repeated or consequential relationship and its exceptions.
3. **Insight:** interpretation of a need, barrier, motivation, or system mechanism.
4. **Opportunity:** improved user progress or outcome without naming a feature.

For each material insight include evidence IDs, counterevidence, alternative interpretation, segment, consequence, confidence, and disconfirming evidence. Do not average away contradictions: investigate segment, timing, channel, and instrumentation differences. Prioritize opportunities by customer consequence, strategic relevance, addressability, and uncertainty without false numerical precision.

**Gate:** Material opportunities have traceable rationale; contradictions and weak segments remain visible. If the pattern is insufficient, return to targeted research.
