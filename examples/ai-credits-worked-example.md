# Chief Product Partner worked example: AI credits through mobile money

**Worked example, 28 September 2026.** The customer interviews, numbers, experiments, costs, and decisions below are **invented to show the process**. They are not findings about Kenyan businesses. The linked provider and payment facts are from public sources checked on this date. The live discovery of this idea was at Research when this example was written, with no customer interviews completed.

The starting idea: “Let small business owners pay about $3 through mobile money for access to several AI tools.” We work together through nine steps. At each step, I do the research and challenge the reasoning; you bring customer access, business choices, and decisions only you can make.

## 1. Frame: what are we trying to change?

**We do:** Pick one group and one job. Start with Kenyan small business owners who make social posts for their businesses. Ask what happened that makes us think their current options fail. We agree what would count as a better result.

**Sample output:** “We want owners who make at least two posts a month to produce useful material without committing to a monthly AI subscription. We think price may be a barrier, but we have not seen purchase attempts or recent work yet. First decision: is there a problem worth solving for this group?” The $3 price and M-Pesa are ideas to test, not facts about what owners need.

## 2. Understand: what could be going wrong?

**We do:** Walk through a recent post: the owner decides what to say, drafts copy, creates or finds an image, edits, and publishes. Consider different reasons they might stop: price, payment method, lack of time, poor output, or infrequent need.

**Sample output:** “If owners already use free AI but cannot make a post they are happy to publish, cheaper credits alone will not help. If they can make good posts but cannot pay for a plan, smaller M-Pesa purchases may help.” We now know what observations would separate those explanations; we still do not know which is true.

## 3. Research: check records, then see real work

**We do:** I check public prices, payment routes, and provider terms. You help reach owners. We ask about the last post they actually made, look at the draft and final version, and include people who use AI and people who do not. We avoid pitching the idea during these conversations.

**Public facts checked:** [ChatGPT has a free plan](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/); [Plus costs $20/month and API use is billed separately](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6950777-what-is-chatgpt-plus). [OpenAI restricts account sharing](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/) and its [business terms restrict account resale and API-key transfer](https://openai.com/policies/may-2025-business-terms/). [Safaricom offers M-Pesa APIs](https://developer.safaricom.co.ke/apis). These facts constrain a possible product; they do not prove demand.

**Invented customer findings for this example:** We speak with 12 owners selected through local business groups, so this is a small convenience sample. Eight used a free AI tool in the last month. Seven struggled to turn the result into a usable post. Five said a paid plan felt too expensive for their level of use. Two described payment trouble. These answers can overlap and do not tell us how common the problem is across Kenya.

## 4. Make sense of what we found

**We do:** Compare what owners said with what they showed us. Look for exceptions. Did those who paid get better results? Did owners who complained about price succeed with free tools?

**Sample output:** “The strongest signal in these 12 conversations is the work needed to turn a draft into a usable post. Price matters to some owners, but payment failure appears in only two stories. A low-cost payment route may help, yet the first opportunity to test is helping owners finish a post.” We keep price and payment as open questions, not dismissed explanations.

## 5. State the bets clearly

**We do:** Write down what must be true for the idea to work and what would change our minds.

**Sample output:**

- Owners will make a usable post more easily with a guided tool than with their current free workflow. If they still need extensive help, this bet fails.
- Some owners will pay $3 for that result even though free AI exists. If they praise a demo but do not pay, this bet fails.
- Enough owners will buy again. If they make one post and never return, a small first purchase is not a business.
- We can cover AI usage, M-Pesa fees, and direct support costs within the pack price. If we cannot, we need a funder, a higher price, or a smaller promise.

## 6. Explore different ways to help

**We do:** Compare options against the problem we saw, the free alternatives, and provider restrictions.

| Option | What it gains | What it loses or risks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sell access to existing subscriptions | Familiar tools | A $3 slice of an account is not a normal subscription; sharing or reselling account access runs into provider restrictions. A formal provider arrangement would need checking. |
| Sell general credits in our own AI app | Flexible, small purchases | Owners still have to turn raw AI output into good marketing material. Free tools compete directly. |
| Sell a guided post-making pack in our app | Focuses on a finished job and permits a clear usage cap | More design and support work; may not suit every marketing task. |
| Provide training and templates for free tools | Fast and low cost to test | Harder to charge $3 per use; little control over the owner’s experience. |

**Sample output:** “Test a guided post-making pack first. The owner pays KES equivalent to $3 through M-Pesa and gets help drafting a small, clearly limited number of posts in our app. We would use permitted provider APIs behind it. This is **not ChatGPT Plus at $3**, and we will not promise access to another company’s app.” We hold off on multiple providers until an owner need justifies them.

## 7. Test it with real payments

**We do:** Build only enough for owners to make posts and pay. Before the test, agree on success rules. Use 15 new owners over two weeks and charge the real $3 equivalent, rather than asking whether they *would* pay.

**Sample rules set before testing:** At least 10 of 15 make a post they consider usable without our help; at least 6 pay; at least 4 make a second paid purchase within 14 days; direct AI, M-Pesa, and support costs stay below $2.25 per $3 pack. No shared provider accounts or transferred API keys.

**Invented result:** 11 of 15 make a usable post without help; 7 pay; 4 pay a second time; average direct cost is $2.10 per pack. All four preset rules pass. This is encouraging for a **small pilot**, not proof of a large market or long-term profit. The sample is small, two weeks is short, and the cost figure excludes fixed costs and tax.

## 8. Decide what to do next

**We do:** Compare results with the rules we set, name the strongest objection, and decide the next commitment.

**Sample output:** “Run a limited pilot of the guided post pack with one customer group and one provider. Do not launch a general AI-credit marketplace or claim a subsidy. Seven of 15 paid in the sample, and four bought again, which clears our small-test rules. The strongest objection is that free tools may become easier to use, and our two-week repeat result could fade. Before the pilot, set a review at 100 paid buyers: if fewer than 25 buy again within 14 days or average direct cost rises above $2.25 per pack, change or stop the offer.” You own the go/no-go decision and the amount to spend on the pilot.

## 9. Describe the first version, if we decide to pilot

**We do:** Turn the pilot decision into a short product brief. Mark anything we have not confirmed.

**Sample output:**

- **Customer:** A Kenyan small business owner making social posts.
- **Promise:** Buy a clearly limited post-making pack in our app using M-Pesa. Show exactly what is included before payment.
- **First version:** Sign in; describe the business, product, audience, and offer; draft and revise copy; create a visual; save or download the result; see remaining pack usage and purchase history.
- **Payment:** Confirm the M-Pesa payment before adding the pack. Handle failed, duplicate, and reversed payments without giving or taking usage incorrectly.
- **Limits:** Cap generations and revisions per pack so the owner knows what $3 buys and costs cannot run away. The exact cap is **still to be set** after real provider and image costs are measured.
- **Checks before pilot:** Confirm the chosen provider’s current terms, real M-Pesa fees, tax treatment, data handling, and the price displayed in KES. These are unresolved, not approvals already obtained.
- **Pilot measures:** Paid buyers, usable posts without help, second purchase within 14 days, direct cost per pack, failed payments, and refund requests.

**Where the real project stands:** We have completed the public-source part of Step 3, but none of the invented customer findings or test results above. The honest next move is to observe recent marketing work with owners. Their evidence could lead to a different product, or show that there is no worthwhile business here.
