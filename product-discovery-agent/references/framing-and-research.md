# Frame, understand, and research

Start each stage with the mandatory memory and reflection checkpoint in SKILL.md. At exit update the discovery record and gate. Ask for evidence rather than treating a fluent explanation as verification.

## 1. Frame

Turn the request into a decision with owner, target population and journey, trigger, desired customer and business outcomes, decision horizon, cost and reversibility, scope, and constraints. Translate fuzzy terms into observable claims: who counts as a user, what behaviour occurred, numerator, denominator, period, source, comparison, and consequence. Record missing baselines as unknown. Separate the source of a signal from the user's explanation for it.

Ask whose problem it is, who defines success, who bears the cost, and whether solving the stated issue would advance the outcome. State at least one competing customer, operational, and measurement frame where material. Do not assume a named solution is the problem.

**Gate:** The decision, population, observable claim, alternatives, and evidence that could change the choice are explicit. The factual claim can remain unverified.

## 2. Understand

Interrogate the terms and causal logic before designing a research program. For each important noun or verb, ask what it means in this context and what distinct customer jobs it may conceal. Distinguish symptom, mechanism, consequence, and proposed remedy. Identify who benefits and what harm a mistaken solution could create. Ask: if we solved the literal request perfectly, would the outcome improve? What else could produce the same observation?

Produce a **provisional** problem model with distinct interpretations, possible causes, observable discriminators, assumptions, and the smallest evidence request to distinguish them. Example: "understand a score" might mean interpret a number, explain a lending decision, identify an error, or decide a next action. Never label this conceptual clarity as empirical verification. Keep questions concrete and stop abstract probing when a testable model exists.

**Gate:** Rival meanings and causal stories can be distinguished by identifiable evidence. Reframe if the decision itself is incoherent.

## 3. Research

For each consequential unknown, specify the decision affected, question, source, method, population/sample, observable result, source limits, and what different findings would change. Work in two passes, looping when needed:

1. **Secondary:** inspect existing metrics, support records, product and partner documents, prior studies, market evidence, and current official external sources. For metrics specify numerator, denominator, period, selection and missingness. A support-contact rate is not automatically a confusion rate.
2. **First-hand:** ask the PM to arrange relevant customer or operator interviews, observation, recent-journey walkthroughs, or approved fieldwork. Compare successful, failed, abandoned, and nonparticipating cases. Ask about actual recent behaviour, workarounds, consequences, and decisions. Do not pitch a feature or claim to have conducted research that the PM has not supplied.

At any stage, give the PM a narrow research request: exact question, primary sources to check, what to bring back (link/date/original evidence/denominator/limits), and why it changes the decision. Offer to search public sources yourself and ask permission when not already authorized. Do not outsource a public lookup merely to avoid doing authorized work. Seek direct evidence for private customer behaviour.

**Gate:** A traceable evidence base can challenge the provisional model, or a concrete collection request identifies why inference must pause. Record uncertainty and bias; do not manufacture prevalence.
